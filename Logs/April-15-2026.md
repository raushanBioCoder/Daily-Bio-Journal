#### 1. Data Sanitization with `.replace()`
I learned that raw genetic data from databases like NCBI often contains "hidden" characters like newlines (`\n`). 
* **The Tool:** `dna.replace("\n", "")`
* **The Logic:** This "washes" the data. If I don't remove these hidden characters, the computer counts them as extra DNA bases, which would make my GC-percentage calculation wrong. In Bio-Coding, clean data is the first step to accuracy.

#### 2. The "Glue" of Life (GC-Content)

* **The Science:** G and C bases are held together by **three hydrogen bonds**, while A and T only have two. 
* **The Insight:** Higher GC-content means the DNA is "stickier" and more stable. My result shows that this section of BRCA1 is perfectly balanced—stable enough to hold its shape, but not so "sticky" that a CRISPR protein couldn't unzip it to make a cut.

#### 3. Breaking Down the GC-Percentage Formula
I spent time today understanding the mathematical ratio that governs DNA stability.

The Equation: $$\text{GC Content \%} = \frac{\text{G count} + \text{C count}}{\text{Total Base Pairs}} \times 100$$
My logic breakdown:

The Grouping (G+C): I add Guanine and Cytosine together because they are the "strong" bases (connected by 3 hydrogen bonds). They represent the structural strength of the gene.

The Division: By dividing by the Total Base Pairs (1,750), I am finding the fraction of the gene that is made of these strong bonds.

The Multiplier (x100): This converts my decimal (0.5246) into a percentage (52.46%).

Conclusion: This percentage is my "Stability Score." Since 52.46% of my "bricks" are made of "steel" (G-C), the gene is stable enough for high-precision CRISPR editing.
